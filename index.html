MileStory

A running community app, conceived, built, and shipped solo.

The problem

Runners track miles constantly, but most apps reduce a training block to a spreadsheet of pace and distance. There's no good way to tell the story of the training, the bad weeks, the breakthroughs, the reason a runner is chasing a particular race. I wanted a place built around that story instead of just the splits.

What I built

A React Native / Expo app on Supabase, with real-time Strava webhook sync that pulls a run in automatically the moment it's logged. Runs become posts inside a "Journey," a running narrative toward a goal, not just a number in a feed. Full social layer underneath it: follows, comments, threaded replies, and a community race database.

My role

All of it. Product decisions, interface design, technical architecture, App Store submissions, the bug fixes at 11pm. No engineering team to defer to, which means every tradeoff was mine to make and mine to live with when it was wrong.

Deciding what's free vs. paid

stays free
  • Core social features, follows & comments
  • Community race database
  • Posting Journeys
premium tier
  • Training plan sharing
  • Full history & deeper analytics
  • Journey Groups, data export

Free stays fully social, since the app only works if enough people are in it. Premium is depth, the things a serious runner wants once they're already hooked, not a paywall on being part of the community.

What I'd tell another builder

Shipping solo strips away the option to hide behind a team's consensus. Every call is visible, and every mistake is yours to catch and fix fast. It made me a faster, more honest product thinker than any meeting ever has.

BRQ

An intake tool I built because the old one was costing everyone time.

At my current company, incoming client requirements moved through a manual, document-based process that was slow and inconsistent, no one owned fixing it, and it kept producing downstream errors. So I designed and built an AI-assisted intake tool to replace it. No roadmap, no directive, just a problem I could see costing real time every week.

I tested it with a few teammates first, adjusted it based on what broke, and it's since been adopted company-wide across the Implementation team, cutting intake time and the errors that used to slip through.
